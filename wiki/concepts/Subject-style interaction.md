# Subject-style interaction

Subject-style interaction means that the effect of a subject choice on generation outcomes depends on the style, or vice versa. Subject concreteness and style abstraction are distinct dimensions: a concrete subject can be rendered in an abstract style.

## Evidence in this wiki

[[Design Guidelines for Prompt Engineering Text-to-Image Generative Models]] (§7) crosses 51 subjects with 31 styles. The authors report a two-way ANOVA with significant subject-category and style-category effects and a significant interaction (all $p<0.01$).

| Style category | Abstract subject | Concrete subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abstract | 3.05 | 3.17 |
| Figurative | 3.49 | 3.54 |

Values are mean five-point joint subject–style representation ratings. Subject concreteness also correlates positively with subject-level mean ratings ($r=0.62$).

Qualitative successes include recognizable symbols for abstract concepts and integration of subject structure with stylistic motifs. Failures include omitted subjects and combinations whose meanings or historical associations are difficult to reconcile. These results do not imply a universal rule that subject and style must share an abstraction level.

## Use in the thesis

Compare modifiers over varied subjects and analyze interaction effects instead of relying only on one aggregate modifier ranking. Evaluate content preservation separately from style fidelity when possible; the source experiment combines them in one score.
