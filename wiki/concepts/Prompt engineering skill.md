# Prompt engineering skill

Prompt engineering skill is the ability to apply language and relevant knowledge to guide a generative model toward a desired outcome. It can include appraisal of candidate prompts, authorship, revision, modifier vocabulary, and knowledge of the surrounding creative workflow.

## Evidence in this wiki

[[Prompting AI Art]] separates appraisal, authorship, and revision in three studies conducted in 2022. Participants’ prompt and image ratings correlate weakly ($r=.29$). Participants write descriptive prompts but seldom use specialist modifiers. After one revision, 33.3% of 150 paired image sets improve overall, 51.3% remain the same, and 15.3% worsen.

The authors interpret this as evidence that effective prompting requires acquired skill. However, a single revision without a training intervention does not measure learning speed, necessary practice time, or an innate-versus-learned distinction. The result should be tied to the sampled people, interfaces, and models.

[[A taxonomy of prompt modifiers for text-to-image generation]] describes community practices and overlapping modifier roles. Together the papers motivate investigating how this vocabulary is learned, but do not establish a curriculum’s effectiveness.

## Thesis use

Separate art expertise, language fluency, model familiarity, and modifier knowledge rather than assuming they form one skill. A useful extension is a multi-iteration study with training and no-training conditions, measured through aesthetics, alignment, and diversity rather than modifier counts alone.
