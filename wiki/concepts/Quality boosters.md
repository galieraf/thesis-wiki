# Quality boosters

Quality boosters are prompt terms used with the intention of improving perceived image quality or detail. The name describes their intended role, not a guaranteed effect.

## Evidence in this wiki

[[A taxonomy of prompt modifiers for text-to-image generation]] (§§4–5) lists examples such as `highly detailed`, `award-winning`, and `trending on artstation`. It notes overlap with [[Style modifiers]]: an artist name can be used for expected detail or finish as well as for a characteristic style. The paper provides ethnographic observations, not controlled estimates of effectiveness.

[[Best Prompts for Text-to-Image Models and How to Find Them]] supplies complementary evidence that an optimized keyword combination can outperform a popularity baseline in aesthetic rankings for Stable Diffusion v1.4. It does not establish that every quality-related keyword is useful individually.

## Thesis use

Distinguish perceived quality from actual output resolution, subject preservation, and diversity. A phrase such as `8k` is a text cue; this taxonomy does not show that it changes the generated image’s pixel dimensions. Use ablations and separate outcome measures to test modifier effects.
