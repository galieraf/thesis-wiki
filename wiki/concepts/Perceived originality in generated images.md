# Perceived originality in generated images

Perceived originality describes how fresh or inventive an image appears to a viewer in relation to its prompt. It is distinct from novelty relative to a training corpus, diversity across a generated set, and legal assessments of copying.

## Evidence from HEIM

[[Holistic Evaluation of Text-to-Image Models]] (§3, §7, Appendix C.1–C.2) asks raters to judge originality on five levels ranging from familiar or overused to highly creative. Art/design scenarios include artist prompts, landing pages, logos, and magazine covers. Openjourney and Dreamlike Diffusion have reported human-originality win rates of 86% and 82%, respectively. These are relative model-ranking summaries, not percentages of demonstrably novel images.

HEIM also applies a LAION watermark detector. Watermark-like output may warrant investigation, but detection does not demonstrate training-data copying or infringement, and absence does not establish originality. The paper acknowledges that general-public judgments can differ from those of artists or legal specialists.

## Thesis use

If modifiers are intended to promote novelty, specify whether the outcome is perceived originality, collection diversity, or similarity to a reference corpus. Do not use one as an unvalidated substitute for another. This is a proposed measurement distinction, not a modifier intervention tested by HEIM.
